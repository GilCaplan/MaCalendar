Where everything runs

Four processes on one machine, plus two clients. The important line is the one that used to be missing: the calendar window is a client too. It records audio and draws results, and posts everything else to the same API the phone talks to.

This was two implementations until recently. The GUI had its own copy of the whole pipeline, and the two drifted: the phone had a sanity pass the Mac lacked, the Mac had a feature the phone lacked, and a fix applied to one was simply absent from the other. 333 lines of duplicated orchestration became about 90 lines of HTTP.

Who decides what

Four kinds of authority run this system, and the useful question about any step is which one is in charge of it. The shape of the answer: the model decides what you meant; deterministic code decides whether that is legal and when it lands.

The workout planner is the pattern in miniature. The model proposes dates; a deterministic policy re-places every one of them against observance and training rules. Its own comment says it: the model proposes, the scheduler disposes.

It knows when Shabbat is

The part that makes this a personal tool rather than a generic one. Sundown is computed from your latitude and longitude, so a repeating event knows to skip Shabbat and yom tov — and knows that the boundary moves through the year.

Where the data lives

All of it outside the repository, in one directory, in formats you can open with ordinary tools. There is no account and no sync service.

The memory is not just a log. Your corrections become the worked examples the LLM is shown next time — a rejected command is never used as one, and a corrected one is shown with your answer as the target. It is the strongest signal in the system.

Nothing leaves the machine

  • Whisper runs on the Apple GPU from a cached model.
  • llama 3.1 answers on localhost:11434.
  • spaCy and the date recogniser are local models.
  • pyluach and astral compute the Hebrew calendar and sundown in pure Python — no lookup service.

The code contains clients for OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini and Microsoft Graph, and none are configured. A test blocks every non-loopback socket and fails the build if that ever stops being true — it had already broken once, when Whisper was resolving its model against Hugging Face on every start.

The one genuine network dependency is the phone reaching the Mac over Tailscale. That is a link between two of your own machines, not a dependency on a service — but with networking off, the phone cannot reach the brain. The Mac alone needs nothing beyond localhost.
